Resume Tailor

Tailor your resume to any job description with AI

Paste a job description. Vignova reads it against your Master Profile, brings forward the experience that posting cares about, and rewrites your summary, bullet points and skills in its language. Nothing is added that is not already in your profile, and you check every change.

Why tailor at all

One generic resume makes the recruiter do the matching

A job description tells you what the employer will look for first. A generic resume leaves that matching to whoever reads it, and a recruiter skimming dozens of applications has little time to find the relevant line on page two.

Tailoring puts the evidence for this role where it will be seen, in the words the posting uses. Done by hand, that means rewriting the summary, reordering bullets and pruning skills for every application. The step-by-step guide to tailoring a resume shows the manual method. The resume tailor does the first draft for you.

Tailoring is not

  • Copying the job description into your resume
  • Adding skills you hope to learn
  • Changing titles to match the posting
  • Repeating keywords until a score goes up
How it works

What happens between the job description and your tailored resume

Tailoring starts from two inputs, your Master Profile and the job description, and ends with a resume you can edit.

  1. Read both inputs

    Your full Master Profile and the complete job description, including requirements and responsibilities.

  2. Select relevant experience

    Decide which roles, projects and achievements to bring forward, and which to shorten or leave out for this role.

  3. Align the wording

    Use the posting's terms where they describe something you have done, so the match is visible to software and to people.

  4. Rewrite the summary

    A short summary aimed at this role, built from your actual background.

  5. Rewrite the bullets

    Turn short profile notes into complete bullet points that lead with the action and include results already in your profile.

  6. Align the skills

    Order and group the skills from your profile so the ones this job asks for come first.

  7. Hand it to you

    Open the result in Resume Studio to review, edit, check against the job and download.

What changes

What tailoring rewrites, and what it never touches

Parts of the resume that tailoring changes or keeps
Part of the resumeWhat tailoring does
SummaryRewritten for the role, from your background.
Experience bulletsRewritten, reordered and trimmed so relevant work comes first.
SkillsSelected and grouped from your profile, with the posting's priorities first.
ProjectsRelevant projects brought forward; unrelated ones may be left out.
Employers, job titles and datesKept exactly as they are in your profile.
Education and certificationsKept as recorded. Nothing is added.
Contact detailsCopied from your profile. A missing field stays empty rather than being guessed.

The rule the resume writer follows: your Master Profile is the only source of fact. It never invents an employer, a title, a date, a degree, a certification, a tool you have not used, or a number that is not in your profile.

Your part is to read the draft. If a bullet overstates something, edit it before the resume goes anywhere.

Illustrative example

A purchasing executive applying for a supply chain analyst role

The profile note and the job requirement below produce the tailored bullet on the right. The figures come from the candidate's own profile.

Profile notes

vendor coordination, POs in SAP MM, 35 vendors. renegotiated freight rates, saved 4.2 lakh per year

The job asks for

  • Vendor management
  • Experience with SAP MM
  • Cost reduction in logistics

Tailored bullet

Managed 35 vendors and raised purchase orders in SAP MM; renegotiated freight rates to cut logistics costs by ₹4.2 lakh a year.

Illustrative example, not a real user's resume. If the profile had not recorded the saving, the tailored bullet would describe the renegotiation without a figure, because the resume writer does not invent numbers.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Will the resume tailor add skills I do not have?

It should not. The resume writer uses your Master Profile as its only source of facts and is instructed never to add an employer, title, date, degree, certification, tool or number that is not in it. Still read every draft before you use it, and delete anything that is not true.

Does tailoring change my job titles, employers or dates?

No. Those facts come from your profile. Tailoring changes what is emphasised and how it is described: the summary, the bullet points, the order and grouping of skills, and which projects are included.

Can I edit the tailored resume?

Yes. It opens in Resume Studio, where every section is editable with a live preview. Save your edits as the same version or as a new one.

How many credits does tailoring use?

One tailoring credit per resume. An application pack, which writes the resume, a cover letter and an application email together, uses one tailoring credit and one writing credit. The free plan includes 3 introductory credits.

Can I tailor my resume to a LinkedIn or Naukri job?

Yes. Paste the job description from any posting. On LinkedIn, Indeed and many company career pages, the Chrome extension can save the posting for you, and on Pro and Premium plans it can start tailoring from the job page.

Do I need a Master Profile first?

Yes. Tailoring works from your profile, so the more complete it is, the better the result. The quickest start is to upload your current resume as a PDF and fill in anything it left out.

Is tailoring the same as keyword stuffing?

No. Keyword stuffing repeats terms without context and makes a resume harder to read. Tailoring describes your real experience in the language the posting uses, where it genuinely applies. See the resume keywords guide.

Should I tailor my resume for every application?

For roles you really want, yes. For a batch of near-identical postings, one tailored version can cover them. The guide to how many jobs to apply for covers the trade-off.
