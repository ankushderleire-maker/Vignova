LinkedIn Headline Examples for Students, Job Seekers and Professionals

LinkedIn headline examples by role and career stage, a formula for writing your own within the 220-character limit, and what to write when you are open to work.

Your LinkedIn headline is the line under your name. It appears in search results, next to your posts and comments, and in connection requests, so it is often the first thing people read about you. If you never edit it, LinkedIn shows your current job title and company, which says little when you are between jobs, changing direction or doing more than your title suggests.

Here are headline examples for students, job seekers and experienced professionals, a formula for writing your own and the mistakes worth avoiding. All examples are illustrative, not real people.

What makes a LinkedIn headline work

  • Role first. Start with the job you do or want. The start of the headline is the part most likely to be seen when it is shortened.
  • Searchable skills. Include two to four skills or specialisms that people hiring for your role look for.
  • One reason to read on. A domain, a result or the kind of problems you solve.
  • Within the limit. LinkedIn allows up to 220 characters. Use the space, but keep it readable.
  • True and consistent. Your headline should agree with your experience section and your resume.

A formula to start from

Role | Skills or specialism | Proof point or focus

Separators such as vertical bars keep several ideas readable in one line. Commas work too.

Students and freshers

  • B.Tech CSE 2026 | Python, Machine Learning, SQL | Seeking data science internships
  • B.Tech CSE 2025 | Backend Developer | Java, Spring Boot, REST APIs | Hackathon finalist
  • MBA Candidate, Marketing | Brand Strategy, Consumer Research, Google Analytics | Summer intern at a D2C brand
  • B.Com Graduate | Accounting and Taxation | Tally Prime, GST, Advanced Excel
  • Design Student | UI/UX, Figma, User Research | Portfolio of six app redesign projects
  • Final-Year Mechanical Engineering Student | CAD, SolidWorks, Manufacturing Processes | Formula Student team member

Software and IT

  • Backend Engineer | Java, Spring Boot, Microservices | Building payment APIs for fintech products
  • Full-Stack Developer | React, Node.js, PostgreSQL | Taking SaaS products from idea to launch
  • DevOps Engineer | AWS, Kubernetes, Terraform, CI/CD | Faster, safer releases
  • QA Automation Engineer | Selenium, Playwright, API Testing | Quality for banking apps
  • Cloud Security Engineer | Azure, IAM, Zero Trust | Securing enterprise workloads

Data and analytics

  • Data Analyst | SQL, Power BI, Python | Turning sales data into weekly decisions for retail teams
  • Data Scientist | Machine Learning, NLP, Python | Forecasting and recommendation models
  • Business Analyst | Requirements, SQL, Process Mapping | Banking and insurance transformation

Finance and accounting

  • Chartered Accountant | Audit, Direct Tax, Ind AS | Helping manufacturing companies close their books on time
  • Financial Analyst | FP&A, Financial Modelling, Excel | SaaS and consumer businesses
  • Accounts Executive | GST, TDS, Tally Prime, Reconciliations | Distribution and FMCG

Sales, marketing and customer success

  • B2B SaaS Account Executive | Enterprise Sales, Negotiation, Salesforce | Mid-market clients in India and the Middle East
  • Performance Marketing Manager | Google Ads, Meta Ads, Analytics | E-commerce growth
  • Content Marketer | SEO Writing, Content Strategy, B2B Tech | Long-form guides for developer audiences
  • Customer Success Manager | Onboarding, Renewals, Account Health | B2B software

HR, operations and other fields

  • Talent Acquisition Partner | Tech Hiring, Employer Branding, Sourcing | Building engineering teams
  • HR Business Partner | Employee Relations, Performance Management, HR Analytics
  • Supply Chain Manager | Procurement, Inventory Planning, SAP MM | Automotive and manufacturing
  • Operations Manager | Process Improvement, Lean Six Sigma Green Belt | Warehousing and logistics
  • Registered Nurse | Critical Care, Patient Education | Multi-speciality hospital experience
  • Mathematics Teacher | CBSE and IB Curriculum, EdTech Tools | Making algebra make sense

Senior professionals and leaders

  • Head of Engineering | Platform and Payments | Growing teams from 10 to 60 engineers
  • Chief Financial Officer | Fundraising, M&A, Governance | Consumer and retail businesses
  • Product Director | B2B SaaS, Pricing, Product-Led Growth | Products for small businesses

Career changers

  • Product Manager | Former Software Engineer | Discovery, Roadmaps, SQL | B2B platforms
  • UX Designer | Formerly in Customer Support | User Research, Figma, Usability Testing
  • Data Analyst | Moving from Finance | SQL, Power BI, Financial Reporting

When you are open to work

If you are looking for a new role, you can say so, but lead with what you do rather than the fact that you are looking:

  • Data Analyst | SQL, Power BI, Python | Open to analyst roles in Bengaluru or remote
  • Recent MBA Graduate | Marketing and Brand Management | Seeking brand or product marketing roles
  • Software Engineer | Java, AWS | Available to join immediately

LinkedIn also has an Open to Work setting that lets you tell recruiters, or everyone, that you are looking. The headline and the setting work together; neither replaces a clear role and skills.

Before and after

A job seeker

Before: Software Engineer | Looking for new opportunities

After: Backend Engineer | Java, Spring Boot, Microservices | Building payment APIs for fintech products

The rewrite says what kind of software, which skills and which domain, all of which someone hiring can search for.

A student

Before: Student at XYZ College

After: B.Tech CSE 2026 | Python, Machine Learning, SQL | Seeking data science internships

The rewrite adds the degree year, three skills and the kind of opportunity the student wants.

Mistakes to avoid

  • "Unemployed" or "Looking for opportunities" on its own. Say what you do first.
  • Buzzwords and titles you cannot back up. "Visionary", "guru" and "ninja" are not what people search for, and they invite scepticism.
  • A string of hashtags or a dozen skills. Keep the headline readable and list the rest in your skills section.
  • Heavy emoji use. One can add personality in some fields, but several make the headline harder to scan.
  • Claims your profile does not support. If your headline says "AI engineer", your experience should show AI work.

Headline, About and skills together

The headline is the summary. Your About section expands on it, and your skills section lists what you want to be found for. When all three use the same core terms, your profile is easier to find and easier to trust.

Frequently asked questions

How long can a LinkedIn headline be?

Up to 220 characters. Only part of it may show in some places, so put the most important words first.

Should I include my current company?

Include it if the company name helps, for example when it is well known in your field. Otherwise the space is better used for skills or your specialism, since your company already appears in your experience.

Should my headline match my job title?

It should be consistent with it, not identical. If your internal title is unusual, such as "Associate II", use the common role name people search for, as long as it describes your actual work.

How often should I update it?

Whenever your role, focus or target job changes, and at the start of a job search.

The short version

Start with your role, add the skills people search for, finish with one reason to read on, and keep it true. If you are looking for work, say what you do before you say you are looking.
