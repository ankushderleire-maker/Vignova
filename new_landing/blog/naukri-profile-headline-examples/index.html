On Naukri, your resume headline is one line that sums up who you are professionally. Recruiters see it with your key skills when they search, so it often decides whether they open your profile. Yet many headlines say little more than "Looking for a job in IT sector".

Below are headline examples for freshers, experienced professionals and career changers, a formula for writing your own and the mistakes that make recruiters scroll past. All examples are illustrative, not real candidates: use them as patterns and write your headline from your own experience.

What makes a good Naukri resume headline

  • It names a role. The job you do or want, in the words recruiters search for.
  • It shows your level. Your degree and year if you are a fresher; your specialism if you are experienced, since your profile already shows your total experience.
  • It includes your core skills. Three or four skills you want to be found for, matching your key skills section.
  • It adds one proof point where there is space: a domain, a certification or a result.
  • It fits the limit. Vignova checks headlines against a 250-character limit. Naukri can change its limits, so watch the counter when you save.

A simple formula

Role or target role | Level or specialism | Three or four core skills | Domain or proof point

You do not need every part. A clear role and three accurate skills already beat most headlines.

Before and after

A fresher

Before: Looking for a job in IT sector. Hardworking, sincere and quick learner.

After: B.Tech CSE 2025 | Backend Developer | Java, Spring Boot, SQL, REST APIs | Built a placement portal during internship

The rewrite names a role and four searchable skills, and replaces qualities no recruiter can verify with a project they can ask about.

An experienced professional

Before: Accounts professional with 6 years experience

After: Senior Accounts Executive | GST Compliance, TDS, Tally Prime, Month-End Closing | FMCG distribution

The profile already shows six years of experience, so the rewrite uses the space for the work and the skills instead.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Availability instead of ability. "Looking for a change" or "Open for opportunities" does not tell a recruiter what you do.
  • Leading with "fresher". Your degree and year already show it. Use the space for the role and skills.
  • Personal qualities. "Hardworking, dedicated, team player" cannot be searched or verified.
  • Too many skills. Fifteen comma-separated skills is a list, not a headline. Keep three or four, and put the rest in key skills.
  • A mismatch with your profile. A headline that mentions Python while your key skills and employment never do raises questions.
  • An old headline. A headline written for your first job, still there five years later.

Naukri headline or LinkedIn headline?

They do the same job on different platforms. Keep the role and core skills consistent, so a recruiter who sees both recognises the same person. LinkedIn allows up to 220 characters; see the LinkedIn headline examples.

Frequently asked questions

What is the character limit for a Naukri resume headline?

Vignova checks headlines against 250 characters. Naukri can change its limits, so check the counter on the form when you edit your headline.

Should freshers write "fresher" in the headline?

It is not needed. Lead with your degree, graduation year and the role you want; together they already tell a recruiter you are starting out.

How often should I update my headline?

Whenever your role, core skills or target job changes, and during a job search, whenever you notice postings using different words for what you do.

Can I use the same headline on Naukri and LinkedIn?

You can use the same role and skills. Adjust the wording to suit each platform's length and style.

Should my headline match my resume?

Yes. The role, skills and experience in your headline should agree with the resume you upload and with the rest of your profile.

The short version

Name the role, show your level, add three or four core skills and, if there is space, one proof point. Keep the headline consistent with your key skills and resume, and replace any wording about availability or personal qualities.
