When to write one

Not every application needs a cover letter

Many applications through job boards in India never ask for one. When a letter is read, though, it can do what a resume cannot: connect your experience to this particular job in a few sentences.

  • The application form has a cover letter field or asks for one
  • You are emailing a recruiter, a hiring manager or HR directly
  • You are changing careers and need to explain why your experience transfers
  • You have a gap, a relocation or a notice period worth explaining
  • You are a fresher and your projects say more than your work history

A cover letter is not

  • Your resume rewritten as paragraphs
  • A list of personal qualities
  • The same letter with the company name changed
What Vignova writes

A short letter with a clear structure

Three to four paragraphs, about 250 to 350 words, with no address or date block, so the same text works as an attachment or in the body of an email.

  1. Greeting

    "Dear Hiring Manager," or a similar opening when no name is known.

  2. Opening

    The role you are applying for and what draws you to it.

  3. Your fit

    One or two paragraphs that connect experience and skills from your profile to the requirements in the job description.

  4. Closing

    A short, professional close that invites the next conversation.

Illustrative example

An opening that earns the next paragraph

An accounts executive applying to a manufacturing company whose posting asks for GST, TDS and Tally experience.

Generic

I am writing to apply for the post of Accounts Executive. I am a hardworking and dedicated person with good knowledge of accounts.

What is missing

  • No evidence behind "good knowledge"
  • Nothing tied to what the posting asks for
  • Could be sent to any company

Specific

For the last three years I have filed monthly GST returns, managed TDS deductions and reconciled bank accounts in Tally Prime for a distribution business, which is the compliance work your Accounts Executive role describes.

How it works

From a saved job to a letter you can send

  1. Complete your Master Profile

    Your experience, projects, education and skills are what the letter draws on. Import your resume to start quickly.

  2. Save the job

    Add the job and its description in the dashboard, or save it from the job page with the Chrome extension.

  3. Generate the letter

    Open the job in Resume Studio and generate a cover letter. It uses one writing credit.

  4. Edit it

    Check every sentence, add something specific about the company, and make the tone yours.

  5. Copy or download

    Copy the text into an email or form, or download a PDF to attach. Your letters stay listed with the jobs they were written for.

Before you send it

A two-minute check

  • The company name and job title are correct
  • Every claim matches your resume and you can talk about it in an interview
  • There is at least one sentence that could only be about this company or role
  • It fits on one page and has no generic filler
  • The PDF has a clear file name, such as Firstname-Lastname-Cover-Letter.pdf

Send it with a tailored resume

A specific letter works best next to a resume written for the same job. The resume tailor rewrites your resume for the job description from your own experience, and the ATS checker shows how it matches.
