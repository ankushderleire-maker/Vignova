How it works

What the scanner looks for

A job description contains two kinds of words: the specific skills and qualifications the employer wants, and everything else. The scanner ignores the everything else.

Skills, tools and certifications

Several hundred common terms, from Python, Power BI and AWS to Tally, GST, payroll, SEO, SAP MM and AutoCAD, including common short forms such as JS or k8s.

Phrases the posting repeats

Multi-word terms that appear more than once, such as "store operations" or "visual merchandising", even when they are not on the skills list.

What counts for more

Specific tools count for more than general skills. Terms listed under requirements, or repeated through the posting, count for more than a passing mention.

  1. Paste your resume

    Copy all of it, including skills listed under each role, not only the summary.

  2. Paste the job description

    The full posting works best, with responsibilities and requirements.

  3. Read the gaps

    Missing terms are listed first by importance. Copy the list and update the resume where the terms apply to you.

Using the results

Put keywords where they carry weight

A term in your skills section tells a reader you claim the skill. The same term in a bullet point shows where you used it. For the skills a posting cares about most, aim for both.

  • Summary: the two or three terms that define the role, such as "financial analysis" and "SAP FICO"
  • Skills section: each relevant tool once, in the posting's spelling
  • Experience and projects: the important terms again, inside what you did and achieved

Limits, honestly

What a keyword scan cannot tell you

  • Whether you are a strong fit. That depends on experience, not vocabulary.
  • Whether a phrase means the same thing in context. "Managed vendor payments" and "accounts payable" may describe the same work.
  • How a particular employer's applicant tracking system is configured.
  • Rare tools mentioned once, which a fixed list may not recognise.
